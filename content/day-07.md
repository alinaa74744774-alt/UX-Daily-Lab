# Day 07 — Weekly Review

## 01 Learn

A weekly review is a short synthesis of what you practiced, what you can explain, and what still needs attention. It turns separate exercises into a clearer picture of your current skills. The goal is not to claim mastery after one week; it is to make the next learning decision more informed.

Review evidence, not memory alone. Revisit your notes and artifacts, such as an interview plan, persona, information architecture, user flow, interface screen, and interactive prototype. Look for decisions you can explain and methods you can repeat. Separate what the work demonstrates from what you only intended to do. For example, a complete flow diagram is evidence that you mapped a path; it is not evidence that real users understood it.

A useful review identifies both progress and friction. Note one strength supported by an artifact, one concept that remains unclear, and one practice habit that would help. Then choose a small next step that addresses the most important gap. This keeps learning focused without turning the review into a list of vague goals.

**Practical principle: make the next step traceable to evidence.** A strong learning priority names the gap, points to an example, and describes an action that can be completed. Avoid choosing a topic only because it sounds advanced or interesting.

## 02 UX English

### 1. Retrospective
**English definition:** A structured review of recent work used to identify what helped, what caused difficulty, and what to improve.  
**UX-context example:** In the project retrospective, the design team identified unclear handoffs as a recurring source of rework.

### 2. Learning Signal
**English definition:** An observable piece of work or behavior that suggests a skill is developing.  
**UX-context example:** Explaining why a navigation label fits the content is a stronger learning signal than simply listing the label.

### 3. Transfer
**English definition:** The ability to apply a learned idea or method in a new situation.  
**UX-context example:** The designer showed transfer by using the same error-recovery principle in a different checkout flow.

### 4. Open Question
**English definition:** An important issue that the available information does not yet answer.  
**UX-context example:** One open question is whether first-time users understand the difference between saving a room and reserving it.

### 5. Next-Step Hypothesis
**English definition:** A small, testable expectation about what practice or change may improve the next result.  
**UX-context example:** My next-step hypothesis is that sketching alternate states before drawing polished screens will reveal missing transitions earlier.

Useful expressions:
- **Evidence from the artifact:** Point to a specific part of the work that supports a reflection.
- **Carry this forward:** Keep using a useful method or lesson in the next project.

## 03 Challenge

### Scenario
You have completed a week of beginner UX exercises across research, personas, information architecture, flows, interface design, and prototyping. The artifacts vary in format and may be incomplete; review only what you actually created.

### Goal
Create an honest, useful summary of your progress and choose one focused practice priority for the next learning cycle.

### Deliverable
A one-page weekly review containing three evidence-based strengths, two open questions or gaps, a brief UX English reflection, and one next-step practice hypothesis.

### Completion Criteria
- Each strength points to a specific artifact or decision from this week.
- The two gaps are concrete and distinguish an unclear concept from unfinished work.
- The English reflection names at least two UX terms used accurately in context.
- The next-step hypothesis includes one action and an observable sign of progress.
- The review does not claim user validation or completed work that did not happen.

## 04 Practice

Estimated time: 35 minutes

1. **Gather the evidence (5 minutes)**  
   Open the work you completed this week. List each artifact and write one sentence about what it demonstrates. Mark missing or unfinished work clearly instead of filling the gap with an assumption.

2. **Identify learning signals (10 minutes)**  
   Select three decisions you can explain. For each one, name the UX principle or method behind it and point to the relevant part of your artifact. Keep the evidence specific.

3. **Name the gaps and English progress (10 minutes)**  
   Write two questions you still cannot answer confidently. Then write a short English paragraph using at least two UX terms from the week. Check that each term describes the work accurately.

4. **Choose a traceable next step (10 minutes)**  
   Pick the gap that matters most for your current progress. Write a next-step hypothesis in this format: “If I [specific practice action], I expect to [observable improvement], because [evidence or reason].” Keep it small enough to complete in one focused session.

## 05 Review

### Self-Checklist
- [ ] Did I review actual work rather than rely only on memory?
- [ ] Can I point to evidence for each strength?
- [ ] Did I record unfinished work and uncertainty honestly?
- [ ] Did I use UX English terms accurately?
- [ ] Is my next step specific and observable?

**Reflection question:** Which piece of work best shows how your UX thinking changed this week, and what will you do differently in your next practice session?
